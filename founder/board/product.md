# Product lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- The experience is easy to picture: a viewer watches a short Thai video, sees a gadget they want, taps the link and buys on Shopee. The path from video to purchase is short.
- Desk-setup and small gadgets are visual. Video shows them better than text does, and the low price (about 100-2,000 THB) makes an impulse buy plausible.
- A faceless format with AI production keeps cost and effort low for one person.
- Affiliate links work before the channel qualifies for ads, so the product can earn from the first videos.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **No one thing, and no reason to watch this channel over the next one.** The brief describes a production method (AI, faceless), not an experience. Thai gadget review content is already crowded. If the videos feel like mass-produced AI output, viewers have no reason to return, and YouTube may refuse monetisation for repetitive, low-effort content.
2. **Corners cut where the viewer can look.** AI voice, stock visuals and AI scripts that never touch the product mean the "review" is not a review. Viewers can tell, and trust is what makes someone buy through a link. Exaggerated claims or missing affiliate disclosure add a policy risk on top.
3. **The experience is handed off across systems nobody on the team controls.** YouTube decides reach, and Shopee decides commission, rules and the buying page. Any change on either side breaks the path from video to purchase, and the founder owns neither. The commission rate per category is unknown.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- In one sentence, what is the one thing this channel does better than any Thai gadget channel? If it needs a paragraph, it is not clear yet.
- What do the first five minutes of a video feel like for a 25-year-old office worker? What does he or she know, or have, by the end that they did not have before?
- What original value is in each video (hands-on testing, comparisons, real photos or footage of the product) that an AI-only pipeline cannot fake?
- What would you cut: Shorts, long videos, or half of the niche?
- Which parts of the craft will you do by hand (script checks, product handling, voice quality), and which are left to automation?
- How will disclosure of affiliate links and AI use look to the viewer, and where does it appear?
- What does a viewer see after clicking the link? Is the product page, price and stock ready for them?
- Open numbers, not answered here: who the channel competes with and what they offer (/founder-competitors); whether viewers in this niche would watch and buy (/founder-consumer); commission per category, revenue per video and monthly break-even (/founder-cfo).

## If FUND IF: the conditions
- Pick one narrow promise (one type of buyer, one kind of product) and state it in one sentence before making more than a small batch of videos.
- Each video must contain original value that a viewer cannot get from the product listing: real testing, real footage or photos, or a clear comparison. Do not publish purely synthetic review claims.
- Disclose affiliate links and AI use plainly in every video and description, and keep product claims honest.
- Make a first test batch (a small number of videos, set by the founder) and check it with real viewers before scaling. The /founder-consumer and /founder-competitors skills should confirm that viewers want this and that a gap exists.
- Get the commission rate and cost numbers from /founder-cfo before any further spend. If the numbers do not work, stop.
