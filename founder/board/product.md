# Product lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- Track A has a clear moment of use: a viewer sees a cheap, useful item, trusts the clip, taps the link. The path from clip to purchase is short, and the links work before any platform threshold.
- Honesty about the limits is the right base: summarising real buyer reviews and saying so, with affiliate disclosure, is a defensible experience and protects trust.
- The cost to start is low (5,000+ THB a month), so a failed test is cheap.
- Both tracks are faceless and can share one production workflow, which keeps the craft problem small if the scope stays small.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. No focus: two tracks, three-plus platforms and one person. Track A (sell something useful) and Track B (be funny) are different products with different audiences and different craft. Splitting a single maker's attention means neither gets good. The Product lens would cut Track B until Track A earns.
2. Interchangeable output. AI voice, AI or stock visuals and review summaries is what everyone in a crowded Thai affiliate niche can make. If the clip feels mass-produced, the viewer has no reason to trust or follow it, and Track B risks YouTube's inauthentic-content policy, which can hit the whole channel. Nothing in the brief says what makes a clip here recognisably better, in taste and in the details nobody sees.
3. The experience after the tap is not owned and not checked. The buyer lands on Shopee, where price, stock, seller quality and the commission rate can change at any time. Clips are made without handling the product, so a bad item or a stale price breaks the trust the clip built. Platform rules on cart or link use are also unchecked. Whether the economics work at all (commission per cheap item) is unknown.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is the one thing the business must do brilliantly, in one sentence? Which track would you cut if you had to?
- Describe the first five seconds and the last five seconds of one Track A clip for a viewer who has never seen you. Why would they trust it over the next clip?
- What is the repeatable format that makes a clip yours and not template output?
- How do you check a product is good, in stock and fairly priced when you cannot touch it, and what do you do when an item turns out bad?
- Who does the viewer blame when the product disappoints: the clip, the seller or Shopee?
- What is the Shopee commission rate and the typical item price? Which platform rules allow links and carts? Unknown; /founder-cfo for the per-sale economics, and the rate itself must be checked on the official Shopee Affiliate terms after your return.
- Who else already makes these clips for the same viewers, and what do viewers complain about? Unknown; /founder-competitors (currently incomplete).
- Would a viewer actually tap and buy, and which objections stop them? Unknown; /founder-consumer.

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Run Track A alone first. Pause Track B until Track A has a defined format and some real result, then revisit.
2. Write the one-sentence promise for the channel and one fixed clip format, with a short quality checklist for voice, visuals, Thai wording, disclosure and claim accuracy.
3. Build a review-checking routine per item (reviews read, price and stock confirmed on the day, no health or hygiene exaggeration) before any clip is published.
4. Finish /founder-competitors and run /founder-consumer on the pitch, to learn who is already in the space and whether viewers would tap.
5. Run /founder-cfo with the real Shopee rate and item prices once the founder is back in Thailand. If commission per sale cannot cover the monthly spend at a believable volume, stop.
6. Review the platform and YouTube policies on AI content and affiliate links before publishing at volume.
